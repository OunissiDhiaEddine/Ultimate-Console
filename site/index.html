Features

Everything a console does, on the PC you already have.

Inspired by the GameScope session on Linux handhelds and HTPCs, built natively for Windows.

One-key gaming mode

Explorer, the taskbar and notifications disappear without signing you out. Steam Big Picture becomes the whole screen, the way a console boots into its dashboard.

The TV becomes the screen

Your other monitors switch off and the TV becomes the only display, at the resolution, refresh rate and HDR setting you choose. Your exact layout comes back when you leave.

TV wakes and sleeps with you

Your Android TV is woken over the network (ADB plus Wake-on-LAN). Put the PC to sleep from Big Picture and the TV sleeps too; wake the PC and the TV comes back on.

Built-in overlay

Master and per-app volume, a task switcher with live window thumbnails, and CPU, GPU and RAM for the selected game. Works with a controller.

Safe by design

A panic hotkey, crash recovery that restores your desktop on restart, and a written list of escape hatches. You never get stuck on a black screen.

A real settings app

Pick your TV display, pair the TV, choose the audio output, record new hotkeys with conflict checks, and manage updates and uninstall in one place.

Hotkeys that stay out of the way

Defaults avoid Windows, Game Bar, NVIDIA, AMD and Steam shortcuts. When you rebind, the app rejects reserved combos and warns about AltGr clashes on AZERTY and Arabic layouts.

Featherweight

Written in Rust. One small background process, no admin rights, and nothing rendered while the overlay is hidden, so it costs next to nothing while you work.

Clean in, clean out

A per-user installer with a Start menu entry and automatic updates. Uninstalling restores your desktop if needed and removes everything: config, logs, keys and the startup entry.

How it works

What happens when you press CtrlAltG

  1. 1

    Your setup is saved

    The current display layout and audio device are written to disk first, so they can always be restored, even after a crash.

  2. 2

    The TV wakes up

    A Wake-on-LAN packet and an ADB wake command go to the TV. The app waits until Windows actually sees the TV before switching.

  3. 3

    The TV becomes the only screen

    Other monitors turn off and the TV becomes primary, with your chosen mode and HDR. Sound can move to the TV too.

  4. 4

    The desktop steps aside

    Explorer closes cleanly, without signing you out and without admin rights. No taskbar, no pop-ups.

  5. 5

    Big Picture takes over

    Steam opens in Big Picture mode as your console dashboard. Grab the controller.

  6. ↩

    Leaving is just as easy

    Exit Big Picture or press the hotkey again: the monitors, sound device and Explorer come back exactly as they were, and the TV turns off.

You'll need

  • A 64-bit Windows PC
  • Steam installed
  • A TV connected by HDMI
  • For wake and sleep: an Android TV on the same network

Setup

Five minutes in the settings app.

Open Ultimate Console from the Start menu. The header shows whether the agent is running, the current mode and whether the TV is reachable.

  1. Go to the Display page. Every connected screen is listed.
  2. Not sure which is which? Press Identify and each screen flashes its number.
  3. Pick your TV as the TV display, then optionally its resolution and Turn HDR on while gaming. A TV that's off may not be listed: turn it on and press Refresh.
  4. If your TV is slow to show up after waking, raise Wait for the TV to appear.
  5. Press Save.
  1. On the TV: Settings → Device preferences → About, and click Build seven times to unlock Developer options.
  2. In Developer options, turn on Network debugging (sometimes called ADB debugging or Wireless debugging).
  3. If your TV has Network standby or Wake on Wi-Fi/LAN, turn it on, so it can be woken from sleep.
  4. In the settings app's TV page tick Control the TV, press Find TV (or type its IP address), then Pair.
  5. The TV asks "Allow debugging?": tick Always allow from this computer and accept. The TV's MAC address is filled in for you.
  6. Press Save, then try Wake and Sleep.
  7. Want the TV to follow the PC's sleep? On the Sleep / Resume page tick Put the TV to sleep when the PC sleeps and Wake the TV when the PC wakes up.
  1. On the Audio page tick Switch the default sound output while gaming.
  2. Pick the TV's HDMI audio device (often named after your graphics card).
  3. Press Save. Your normal speakers or headset come back when you leave gaming mode.
  1. The Hotkeys page lists gaming mode, overlay and panic restore.
  2. Press Record and type the new shortcut. Esc cancels.
  3. The app checks it on the spot: shortcuts reserved by Windows, already used by another program, the same as another hotkey, or that type a character with AltGr are flagged.
  4. Reset brings back the default. Press Save when everything says OK.
  1. In Steam open Settings → Controller → Guide Button Chord Layout.
  2. Add a chord you like (for example Guide + Y) that sends the keyboard shortcut CtrlAltO.
  3. Now the overlay opens from the couch. Navigate it with the stick or D-pad, no keyboard needed.

Ready? Press CtrlAltG

Or use Enter gaming mode at the top of the settings app. To leave, exit Big Picture or press it again.

Help

Getting your desktop back, always.

Gaming mode closes Explorer, so here's what to do if something goes wrong, in order.

1. Press the panic hotkey

CtrlAltShiftR restores the display layout, audio device and Explorer from any state.

2. Exit Big Picture

Use Steam's menu to exit Big Picture. The agent notices and restores everything.

3. Restart the agent, or sign out and back in

If the agent crashed mid-game, Windows restarts it and it restores your desktop on its own. Signing out and back in does the same.

4. Start Explorer by hand

Press CtrlShiftEsc to open Task Manager, choose Run new task, type explorer and press OK. The taskbar comes back.

5. Monitors still off?

Press WinP and pick Extend or PC screen only, or run displayswitch /extend from Task Manager.

How do I uninstall?

Settings → Apps → Installed apps → Ultimate Console → Uninstall, or the Uninstall button in the settings app. It restores the desktop if needed and deletes everything it created.

The overlay doesn't show over my game

Overlays can't draw over exclusive fullscreen. Switch the game to borderless or windowed fullscreen (most modern games already are). Games running as administrator also can't be controlled from the overlay.

Where are the settings and logs?

Config: %APPDATA%\UltimateConsole\config.toml. Logs (kept 7 days): %LOCALAPPDATA%\UltimateConsole\logs\. The settings app's About page has an Open logs folder button.
